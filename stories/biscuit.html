Dog

Biscuit

The only one not barking on a rainy Tuesday.
2,920 days together

He was at the Humane Society on a rainy Tuesday. The kennels were loud — every dog announcing itself, demanding attention, selling their case for a home. Biscuit sat quietly in the back corner. The only one not barking.

Every morning after that, he dragged his blanket to the back door and waited. Rain or shine. It became the rhythm of the house — before coffee, before breakfast, before anything else, there was Biscuit at the door with his blanket, looking out at the yard like he was on duty.

He always knew when someone was sad. Would just come sit on their feet.

He had a way of reading a room that no one ever taught him. When someone was having a hard day, he didn't paw or whine or try to fix it. He just walked over, settled himself on their feet, and stayed there. Heavy and warm and certain.

For seven years, he never climbed into bed. He had his spots — the blanket by the door, the corner of the living room, the patch of sun by the window — and he kept to them. Then came the night of the big storm. Thunder rolled in from the west and the house shook with it. Biscuit appeared in the bedroom doorway, hesitated for a moment, then climbed up and pressed himself against the pillows. He was seven years old. It was the first time.

Eight years of mornings at the door. Eight years of knowing. Eight years of quiet companionship.

Some dogs announce themselves. Some demand the spotlight. Biscuit was never that. He was the one who waited quietly, who knew without being told, who showed up in the doorway during a storm and decided it was finally time. The blanket still sits by the back door. The morning light still comes through the same window. The house still remembers the weight of him on someone's feet when the day felt too heavy to carry alone.
